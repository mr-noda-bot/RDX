§1 Idempotency Rules

These rules define what it means for a spec to be in canonical form. They apply to this document and to every document that adopts this header.

IDEMPOTENCY CONDITIONS — all must hold
I-1 Granularity is lossless. Re-application does not compress, summarize, or abstract away any detail present in the current form. If a re-application would reduce granularity, it is rejected and the reduction is logged as a divergence event, not silently applied.
I-2 Semantic labels are context-local. A term that appears in both Track A and Track B carries its Track A meaning in Track A sections and its Track B meaning in Track B sections. The header declares the scope boundary. Re-application checks that no term has migrated across the boundary without an explicit exchange arrow.
I-3 Tracks are separated and explained separately. Track A content is never presented as a restatement of Track B content and vice versa. Each track has its own derivation logic, its own failure modes, and its own accounting. The only permitted cross-track statement is a reconciliation event or an exchange arrow — both are labeled as such.
I-4 Reconciliation is convergence provenance, not merger. A reconciliation event records: (a) what Track A produced from its derivation, (b) what Track B produced from its integer structure, (c) that they are the same thing arrived at independently, (d) the depth d at which the convergence is visible. After logging, both tracks continue independently. The reconciliation column does not absorb either track.
I-5 Exchange arrows are non-constitutive. A guidance arrow from Track A to Track B (or reverse) is an invitation to investigate, not a proof. The receiving track must derive the result in its own terms for it to count as knowledge in that track. Until derived, an exchange arrow is marked as a pointer, not a result.
I-6 POV is derived at read time. No document version carries a fixed POV. The POV is computed from d at the moment of reading. What is visible changes with depth. A document that hard-codes a POV has violated idempotency — each reader at each depth sees what their depth makes visible, no more and no less.

§4 POV — Depth-Indexed Point of View

POV is not carried by an operator or a document version. It is derived at read time from the depth d at which the reader is positioned.

POV Derivation — Canonical Form
POV(d) = R^d(0⁺) − R^(d−1)(0⁺)
The discrete derivative of the depth sequence. At d=0: undefined — no inside, no outside, no POV possible. At d=1: POV(1) = R(0⁺) − Q(0) = the first finite value minus the undefined = the first inside. The undefinedness of Q(0) is not a gap — it is the load-bearing asymmetry that makes the d=1 term non-trivial.

This is Axiom D applied to the self-consistency structure. POV inherits all of Axiom D's properties: defined on adjacent pairs, no continuous embedding required, natural integrator for the self-consistency structure just as Ld_der is the natural integrator for discrete semantic dynamics. They are the same object at different layers.
RULE-POV-01 — POV is Placeable

To place a recursion tracker or POV at any point in the system, specify d. The POV at that point is derived — not assumed, not carried by an operator. The inside of the structure at depth d is the incremental self-consistency gain from d−1 to d.

RULE-POV-02 — POV Reads the Reconciliation Column

The same reconciliation event looks different from different depths. At d=1, the convergence is visible as a structural fact. At d=3, the derivation chains that produced the convergence are visible too. POV does not change what is in the reconciliation column — it changes how much of the provenance chain is legible from that position.

§6 Usage — Applying This Header to a New Spec
STEP 1 — Fill the canonical header block

Copy the header block from §0. Fill in SPEC-ID, TRACK A (name, accounts-for, semantic scope, unique content), TRACK B (same), RECONCILIATION (current count), EXCHANGE (pointer count), POV (current d and what is visible), FORM (idempotency statement for this specific spec).

STEP 2 — Body content is track-labeled

Every section in the body carries a track label: TRACK-A, TRACK-B, RECONCILIATION, EXCHANGE, or POV. No unlabeled content. Unlabeled content cannot be checked for idempotency.

STEP 3 — Re-application protocol

To re-apply: diff the current document against the header block field by field. Any field that has changed is a divergence event. Log the divergence with: which field, what changed, which track is affected, whether granularity was lost, whether a semantic term migrated tracks. Do not silently absorb the divergence.

STEP 4 — Version only on structural change

Content updates within the existing structure do not increment the version. Version increments when the structure of the header block itself changes — new fields, removed fields, changed idempotency rules. Content is always granular; structure is versioned.
