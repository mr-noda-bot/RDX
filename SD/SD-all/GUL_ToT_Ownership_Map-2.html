$$S = (s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_n) \in \mathcal{G}$$

$$P_i \in \mathcal{G}^* \qquad \text{(momenta live in the dual space)}$$

Axiom D — GUL Derivative Operator

Semantic Derivative $D$

The derivative operator $D$ is defined internally on $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G})$, the space of all functionals over $\mathcal{G}$. Two forms are provided — use whichever matches the structure of $\mathcal{G}$:

Continuous form:

$$D[f](S) := \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \frac{f(T_\epsilon(S)) - f(S)}{\epsilon}$$

Discrete form (canonical for ToT trees):

$$D[f](S_n) := f(S_{n+1}) - f(S_n), \qquad S_{n+1} = T(S_n)$$

Canonical momentum is derived through $D$ and lands in $\mathcal{G}^*$: $$P_i := \frac{D[L_{\text{der}}]}{D[\dot{s}_i]} = \frac{\partial L_{\text{der}}}{\partial \dot{s}_i} \in \mathcal{G}^*$$ Semantic meaning: $P_i$ measures the sensitivity of the action functional $A[S]$ to perturbations in the $i$-th thought transition rate. $$H_{\text{der}}(S, P) := \sum_i P_i \dot{s}_i - L_{\text{der}}(S)$$

Hamilton's equations of motion:

PB_der

Poisson / Symplectic Mechanics

Axiom SP — Symplectic Structure on $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{G}^*$
There exists a canonical Poisson bracket on $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{G}^*$ satisfying three properties, all as internal axioms:

(i) Antisymmetry: $\{f, g\}_{\text{der}} = -\{g, f\}_{\text{der}}$
(ii) Leibniz rule: $\{f, gh\}_{\text{der}} = g\{f,h\}_{\text{der}} + h\{f,g\}_{\text{der}}$
(iii) Jacobi identity: $\{f,\{g,h\}_{\text{der}}\}_{\text{der}} + \{g,\{h,f\}_{\text{der}}\}_{\text{der}} + \{h,\{f,g\}_{\text{der}}\}_{\text{der}} = 0$

This replaces the classical Darboux theorem. The canonical relation $\{s_i, P_j\}_{\text{der}} = \delta_{ij}$ follows from these axioms — it is a consequence, not a validation example.

The Poisson bracket, fully internal to $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{G}^*$:

$$\{f,\, g\}_{\text{der}} := \sum_i \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial P_i} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial P_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial s_i}\right)$$

Time evolution of any functional $f \in \mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G})$:

$$\dot{f} := D[f](\mathcal{G}) = \{f,\, H_{\text{der}}\}_{\text{der}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}$$
AA_der

Action-Angle Variables

Precondition — Liouville–Arnold (ToT-Native)
$\text{AA\_der}$ exists if and only if there exist $n$ independent functionals $F_1, \ldots, F_n \in \mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G})$ in involution: $$\{F_i,\, F_j\}_{\text{der}} = 0 \quad \forall\, i, j$$ This is the ToT-native replacement for the classical Liouville–Arnold theorem. When this precondition does not hold, the transform $(I, \theta)$ is undefined. Asserting AA_der without verifying this condition for the specific $H_{\text{der}}$ in use is an error.

Canonical transformation, derived from $(S, P)$:

$$(I,\, \theta) := D[\text{AA}](S,\, P)$$

Under this transformation, $H_{\text{der}}$ depends only on action variables:

$$H_{\text{der}} = H_{\text{der}}(I), \qquad \dot{I} = 0, \qquad \dot{\theta} = \frac{\partial H_{\text{der}}}{\partial I}$$

$$\dot{s}_i := D[s_i](\mathcal{G}) = \{s_i,\, H_1,\, H_2\}_{\text{der}}$$

Discrete / Variational Integrator

$$L_{d,\text{der}}(S_n,\, S_{n+1}) := D[L_d](S_n,\, S_{n+1})$$

$$D_2\, L_{d,\text{der}}(S_{n-1},\, S_n) + D_1\, L_{d,\text{der}}(S_n,\, S_{n+1}) = 0$$
